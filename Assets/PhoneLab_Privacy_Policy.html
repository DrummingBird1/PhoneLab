PhoneLab

Privacy Policy

Effective & last updated: October 30, 2026

In short

PhoneLab's own code never connects to the internet. There are no PhoneLab servers, no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. Every sensor reading is calculated and shown on your device, and nothing is sent to the developer.

PhoneLab comes in two builds. The GitHub / F-Droid build has no internet permission at all. The Google Play build adds Google Play Billing for an optional tip jar. That gives the app internet access, so that Google can process a tip if you choose to leave one, and Google's billing library can send Google diagnostic logs about its own operation. PhoneLab never sees your payment details.

PhoneLab only creates files when you ask for them (a CSV recording or a spec export). They stay on your device until you share them yourself.

Who publishes PhoneLab

PhoneLab (package id com.sensolab.devicemonitor) is developed by an independent developer. It is distributed on Google Play and as an APK on GitHub Releases, F-Droid, IzzyOnDroid and other app stores. PhoneLab is a hardware-monitoring tool, so it has to read sensors, location and microphone levels to work. This policy explains what it does with them.

PhoneLab is open source under the GPL-3.0 licence. You can check every statement here against the source code on GitHub.

Data PhoneLab collects

None. The developer does not collect, receive, store, sell or share any personal data, in either build. PhoneLab contains no analytics, advertising or crash-reporting software of any kind. Apart from Google Play Billing in the Google Play build, every library in the app is a standard AndroidX or Material Components library for the user interface and for scheduling on-device background checks. None of them sends data off your device.

Everything described below happens locally, in your device's memory or storage, using Android's own sensor and file APIs.

What PhoneLab reads on your device

  • Hardware sensors: accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer, light, pressure, proximity, humidity, temperature, hinge angle and similar. These need no permission. Readings are shown on screen and used by the Tools tab (level, compass, metal detector, vibration analyzer).
  • System and hardware information: device model, Android version, CPU, GPU, memory, storage, display, battery state and thermal-zone temperatures, read from Android's APIs and from system files that any app can read. This feeds the System and Hardware tabs, the benchmark, alerts, the widget and the Quick Settings tile.
  • Location, microphone, heart rate and steps, only after you grant the matching permission (see the table below).
  • Biometric availability: the Hardware tab shows whether your phone has a fingerprint or face sensor and whether one is set up. PhoneLab never asks you to authenticate and never has access to biometric data.

PhoneLab does not read your phone number, IMEI, serial number, advertising ID, Android ID, accounts, contacts, photos, messages or list of installed apps.

Data stored on your device

PhoneLab keeps a small amount of data in its private app storage, so it stays the way you set it up:

  • Your settings: theme, language, units, display mode and alert thresholds.
  • Your custom alert rules and the results of your last Device check.
  • If you turn on battery history in Settings: one battery level and temperature sample about every 15 minutes, for the last 24 hours only. "Clear history" in Settings deletes it.

Uninstalling PhoneLab, or tapping Clear storage in Android's app settings, deletes all of it. Nothing is ever sent to the developer.

Android backup. If Android's own backup is turned on, Android can include PhoneLab's settings, alert rules and battery history in your device backup to your Google account. On Android 9 and later this backup is end-to-end encrypted with your screen lock. Android and Google handle it, and the developer cannot access it. Exported files and Device check results are excluded from backups.

Files you create

Three features produce something you can keep or send, and you start all of them yourself:

  • Sensor recording: tap Record on the Sensors tab to save sensor readings to a .csv file. If location access is granted, the file also includes GPS position rows.
  • Spec export: tap Export on the System tab to save your device's specifications as a .txt or .json file. This file names your device model and hardware, so sharing it can identify the kind of phone you use.
  • Device check report: a pass/fail text summary that you can share from the Device check tab. It is not saved as a file.

Files are saved in PhoneLab's own folder on your device (Android/data/com.sensolab.devicemonitor/files/Documents, or the app's cache as a fallback), and Android deletes them when you uninstall the app. PhoneLab never uploads them. A file or report leaves your device only if you send it yourself with the Android share sheet. From then on, the app you share it to handles it under its own policy.

The tip jar (Google Play build only)

The standard build (GitHub, F-Droid and other stores) has no tip jar and no Google libraries. Nothing in this section applies to it.

In the Google Play build, the About tab has an optional "Support development (tip)" button. Tips are one-time purchases that unlock nothing. When you tap the button:

  • PhoneLab connects to Google Play Billing, through the Google Play Store app on your phone, to show the tip prices and open Google Play's own purchase screen.
  • Google processes the payment. PhoneLab never sees your card number, billing address or Google account details. On your device, the app receives only a confirmation for the tip (the product and a purchase token). It uses this to complete the purchase and say thank you, and doesn't store or forward it.
  • Like every seller on Google Play, the developer receives the standard order records that Google provides in Google Play Console, such as order number, product, price, date and the buyer's country. They are used only for accounting and refunds.
  • Since version 6, the Google Play Billing Library logs information about its own operation, such as whether billing calls succeed and any connection problems. It sends these logs to Google through Google's datatransport component, and Google uses them to improve the library and support errors. In PhoneLab this only starts once you open the tip jar; logs already queued may be uploaded in the background shortly afterwards. PhoneLab and its developer do not receive these logs.

Google handles this data under the Google Privacy Policy and the Google Play Terms of Service, which also cover downloading PhoneLab from Google Play in the first place. If you never tap the tip button, the Google Play build connects to nothing at all.

No accounts, no ads, no analytics

PhoneLab has no sign-in and no user accounts, and keeps no profile about you anywhere. It shows no advertising and includes no advertising or analytics libraries. The only third-party code in either build that can use the network is Google Play Billing in the Google Play build, described above.

Links that leave the app

The About tab's privacy policy, source code, PhoneLab Web and Rate links open in your browser or the Play Store app, and the feedback button opens your email app. What happens there is covered by those apps and websites, not by PhoneLab. PhoneLab Web is a separate website. This policy covers the Android app only.

Your choices and your data

  • You can deny or revoke any permission. Only the feature that needs it stops working.
  • You can delete everything PhoneLab stores by uninstalling it or clearing its storage in Android's settings.
  • The developer holds no personal data about you, so there is nothing to request, correct or delete on the developer's side. For tip purchases and billing logs held by Google, use your Google Account controls and Google Play's order history.
  • If you want an app that cannot reach the internet at all, install the standard build from GitHub Releases or F-Droid.

Children's privacy

PhoneLab is rated for Everyone. It does not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children, because it collects no information from anyone of any age. In the Google Play build, tips go through Google Play's purchase flow, which applies any parental controls or purchase approvals set on the Google account.

Changes to this policy
